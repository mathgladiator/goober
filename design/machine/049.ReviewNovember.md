## 4. A second bypass the sweep found: `kProject`'s `rev`

Fixing M1 meant sweeping every scope rule that reads a number. `kProject` read `rev` only when
it was an int64, and otherwise checked the project at its head. But `source_deploy` and
`source_build` read `rev` as a number or a string of digits (host ids are strings in JSON).

Scenario: project P's head is in the operator's scope, but revision 2 of P declared a box
serving another tenant's domain. A focused operator calls `source_deploy {"project": "P",
"rev": "2"}`. The rule checked the head and passed; the tool built revision 2 and deployed a box
that could write the other tenant's data.

Fix: `kProject` reads `rev` with `mcp::integer_arg`, refuses a `rev` of any other shape, and
checks the revision the tool builds. The operator MCP's type normalization (M1) now also makes
`"2"` arrive as `2`. Test: `ScopeTest.ADeployIsCheckedAtTheRevisionItBuildsWhateverShapeRevHas`.

The other rules (`kTenant`, `kBox`, `kDomain`, `kManifest`, `kSchema`, `kTicket`) read strings
the way their tools do, and were found sound.

**Proposal for N4.** A refusal about something outside the operator's scope says only that:
"box X is outside your scope", "this box would serve a tenant outside your scope", "schema S is
bound by a box outside your scope", "that ticket is outside your scope". An unknown box gets the
same refusal as one outside the scope, so a focused operator can't tell which names exist. The
operator's own arguments may still be echoed (a domain or tenant they named themselves). The
full reason, with the tenant, goes to the audit log, where the host's operators read it. A test
per rule: a focused operator's refusal never contains another tenant's name or domain, and an
unknown box and another tenant's box read the same. Small, and it touches only
`operator_scope.cpp` and its test.

## 6. Decisions for the human

Nothing changed for these. Each is a behavior the reviewers found deliberate but worth a
person's yes or no.

1. **Box route limits can loosen an operator's API control and the host defaults.** 047 § 1
   orders the levels host defaults < domain < API < a box route's `limits` < an endpoint
   override, the most specific winning per property. So a box's manifest saying
   `"rate": {"per-minute": 6000}` on a route beats an operator's `box:<name>` API control of 60 a
   minute until the operator adds an endpoint override. A box author, or an agent deploying a
   box, can raise what an operator set. Options: (a) keep it, as built; (b) box limits only
   tighten: each property of a route's `limits` applies as the lower of it and the levels below,
   and only an operator's endpoint override can loosen. Recommendation: **(b)**. Operators
   would still beat a box at any time, and a manifest could no longer undo an operator's
   control.
2. **An agent is an OAuth client plus a person.** On `/mcp` the strict rate and the queue key
   on (client, person), so a person who registers N clients gets N buckets. Options: keep it, or
   key by person alone. Recommendation: **keep it**. Each client needs the person's consent and
   shows on their account page with a revoke, and keying by
   person alone would let one bad agent starve that person's good ones.
3. **Heat studies never wait for approval.** `is_read_call` answers true for `heat_study_start`
   and `heat_study_stop` (048 § 2), though a study adds a little work to every request of its
   domain for up to a day. Bounded: one per domain, 4 at once, about 290 ns a request.
   Recommendation: keep it; say so if a study should wait like a changing tool.
4. **Attachments count toward the daily upload budget and can delay increments.** Attachments
   and the nightly are never held back (019 § 9), but their bytes are counted. A day of large
   uploads can push the database increments and log archives past
   `limits.upload-bytes-per-day`, so they wait a day, and the restore points between nightlies
   reach S3 a day late. Options: keep it, or leave attachments out of the count. No recommendation
   beyond naming it: attachments are the person's data and are uploaded once; increments are
   the finer restore points.
5. **Local copies drop to 3 when S3 is on, even if uploads fail.** `goober-host backup` keeps 3
   dated copies whenever `backup.s3` is set, whether or not a nightly ever reached the bucket.
   With wrong keys or a missing bucket, the host has 3 nights of history instead of 14.
   Recommendation: **keep 14 until a nightly reached S3 in the last two days**, then 3.
6. **An operator's control that lifts `fuel` skips the route's own `limits.fuel`.** With
   `fuel: "off"` (sent to the call as `goober-control-fuel: 0`), `host.cpp` gives the call the
   box's `behavior.fuel`, not the route's `limits.fuel`. 047 § 3 says "off" gives the box its own
   fuel back; it doesn't say which of the box's two. Options: the route's `limits.fuel` when it
   has one, else `behavior.fuel`; or as built. Recommendation: the route's own, since "the box's
   own" most naturally means the most specific thing the box said.

## 7. Checked and found sound

- **Controls.** Refusals say only "rate limit reached" (or the closed or body answer) and name
  no control; `observe` counts and never refuses; overrides are compiled and sorted once per
  change; at most 100 overrides and 50 API controls per domain; `timeout-ms` only shortens a
  call; every `control_set` and `control_remove` is audited and in the change log; observers
  can't change controls; the panel's edits go through the same checks as the tools.
- **Box route limits.** Checked at upload against the host's `limits.fuel` and
  `limits.body-max-bytes` (and, since C6, again on a config change); only the keys the route's
  kind takes; no `"off"`; `timeout-ms` refused with a pointer to the route's own.
- **MCP queue.** Every way out releases its slot: the finish, a dropped finish, the wait limit, a
  client gone while waiting, the pool refusing, the host stopping; a call held for approval
  holds no slot; a flood from one agent doesn't starve another or the pages; a quarter of the
  box threads stay free of MCP.
- **Heat studies.** Keyed hashes only, no addresses or user ids; host-internal paths not
  studied; reports kept 30 days; scope-checked by tenant (since M1, for every shape of id).
- **Limits.** Over budget, the derived and expiring are dropped oldest first and new tries,
  exports, and snapshots are refused naming the limit; snapshots are never deleted by the host.
- **Box-version keeping.** Never the live version, a disabled box's, the newest 3 that were
  live, or one a kept change set names; a deleted version leaves a tombstone.
- **Log velocity and the upload budget.** Every line counted by log and writer; the nightly and
  attachments are never held; the budget resets daily.
- **Speed.** Pages at 20,300 req/s from one client process and about 44,000 from six; a heat
  study and enforcing controls cost nothing measurable; the report tools answer in under 3 ms.
