# Security Review, 2026-10-26

> Status: machine-authored. Three read-only reviewers each took one part of `host-src/`
> (identity, OAuth, and the screens; content, rendering, and files; the box boundary, SQL,
> operators, and the HTTP server) and reported confirmed findings with attack scenarios,
> suspicions, and what they found sound. This records every finding and what became of
> it. Fixes landed in commits f4db06f, 921e313, and the one that adds this file, and each
> has a test. Nothing here has had an outside review; this is the house checking itself.

## Fixed

| # | severity | finding | fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | critical | A box answering 1xx, 204, or 304 with a body made Beast throw on a network thread and ended the process. | Boxes may answer 200 to 599 only; the server drops bodies on bodiless statuses and turns any response it can't frame into a 500. |
| B2 | high | The "one statement" check scanned past the end of the box's SQL text, which sits in box memory with no terminator; at the edge of memory that read the guard page and crashed the host. | The scan stops at the end of the text. |
| B3 | high | SQL could build values of up to 1 GB (`s || s` doubled 29 times) within the step budget, and the result was copied three times before any cap; a `bad_alloc` ended the process. | `SQLITE_LIMIT_LENGTH` of 4 MiB, results capped at 8 MiB as they are built, and every job catches exceptions. |
| B4 | high | wasmtime tables, and the counts of tables, memories, and instances, were unlimited; `table.grow` could make the host allocate gigabytes for one unit of fuel, also at upload. | One instance, one memory, one table of at most 10,000 elements, multi-memory off, in calls and in the dry instantiation. |
| C1 | high | Markdown nested 8,000 deep overflowed the stack and crashed the host on every visit to the page. | Blocks nest at most 32 deep; deeper markers are text. |
| I1 | medium-high | A script on a tenant's domain (a box's HTML, a document page) could register an agent client and POST the consent form with `fetch()`, getting agent tokens without the person clicking Allow. | Consent and device POSTs must be the person's own navigation: `Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate`, `Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`, and `Sec-Fetch-User: ?1`, which scripts can't forge. The person is mailed whenever an app or agent is allowed. The full fix is a separate origin (Q24). |
| B5 | medium | `REPLACE INTO` and `INSERT/UPDATE OR REPLACE` delete the row they collide with, and SQLite's authorizer reports only an insert or an update, so a strategy with `insert` alone could overwrite rows. | Refused for boxes, with a pointer to `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`. |
| B6 | medium | SQL runs inside a host call, where the request's epoch deadline can't reach it, and `step-budget` had no ceiling. | Every statement stops after 2 s; step budgets are capped at 100M; LIKE patterns at 1,000 bytes. |
| B7 | medium | `/mcp` ran JSON-RPC batches: thousands of tool calls for one rate-limit token, with only the last one audited. | Batches are refused. |
| B8 | medium | Connections and the box queue were unbounded. | At most 4,096 connections; the box queue holds 256 jobs per thread, then answers 503. |
| C2 | medium | The inline markdown scans were quadratic: `[a](` x 20,000 took 16 s per request. | One work budget per document for the scans that can go quadratic; what's left past it stays text. |
| C3 | medium | Malformed front matter failed open: a bad JSON `requirements` line, an unclosed block, or `draft: True` made a private page public. | A page whose front matter has any error answers 500 and is never listed; `draft` must be a boolean; `.html` pages use the parser's trimmed `---` rule. |
| C4 | medium | Box JSON rendered through a template had no CSP, and kept the box's own `cache-control`, so one person's data could land in a shared cache. | The templated CSP, `Vary: accept, cookie`, and `private, no-cache` whenever someone is signed in. |
| I2 | medium | `return=/%09/evil.com` got past `safe_return`, since browsers strip tabs: an open redirect after sign-in. | `safe_return` refuses every byte at or below space, DEL, and backslash. |
| I3 | medium | Sign-in codes allowed about 25 guesses per address per 10 minutes from many machines: roughly a 10% chance per month at an operator's address. | 20 wrong guesses per address per day, across all its codes. |
| I4 | medium | The origin check ignored the scheme, so a page injected on plain `http://` could post to `https://`. | Origin is compared as scheme, host, and port; a cookie POST without Origin or `Sec-Fetch-Site` is refused; HSTS once on TLS. |
| I5 | medium | A tenant's `sign-in.css` could hide "(unverified)" or relabel the buttons on the consent screens. | Consent and device screens don't load it. |
| I6 | low-medium | `/oauth/authorize` redirected errors to a self-registered client's URI before consent: an open redirector. | Dynamic clients get error pages before consent. |
| I7 | low | Access-log extras overwrote the line's own `status`. | Extras never overwrite base fields. |
| I8, C7 | low | `Host: site.com:443@evil.com` resolved to site.com and then lent its text to issuers, metadata, and feed URLs. | A malformed Host header is a 400. |
| I9 | low | Changing a client from person to agent didn't affect its existing refresh tokens. | A refresh takes the agent flag from the client as it is now. |
| I10 | low | No session list on the account page; codes in the dev outbox; operator timing. | Browser sessions are listed with End; all mail, dev included, goes through the background queue. |
| B9 | low-medium | Logs grew without bound. | Logs rotate at 64 MiB, keeping three; logged paths are capped at 512 bytes. |
| B10 | low | A committed WAL bundle that failed to apply could be truncated away, or stop the host from starting. | The log isn't truncated while a bundle is unapplied; recovery sets a failing bundle aside in `wal/failed/` and starts; names over 255 bytes are refused up front. |
| B11 | low | The SQL authorizer was removed after prepare, so a re-prepare after a schema change went unchecked. | It stays on until the statement finishes (the host's own BEGIN runs without it). |
| B12 | low | A types document of shared `$ref`s expanded exponentially (2^30) at upload and in `tools/list`. | The cycle walk is memoized and documents expanding past 10,000 nodes are refused. |
| B13 | low | Files were created with the default umask. | The host runs with `umask 077`. |
| C6 | low-medium | The image fetch let curl expand URL globs (an internal scan) and read `~/.curlrc` and proxy settings; its pipes could deadlock. | `curl -q -g --noproxy '*'`, run through `run_process`, which drains both pipes with `poll`. |
| C8 | low | `Accept: text/markdown` returned a fragment's HTML as `text/html` without a CSP. | Served as `text/plain`. |
| — | low | A header value with CR or LF (a front matter `redirect`, say) could reach the wire. | The server refuses any header holding CR, LF, or NUL. |
| — | low | Mustache output was unbounded. | 8 MiB and 2,000 partials per page. |
| — | low | IPv6 clients could rotate addresses past rate limits. | Rate limits key IPv6 by /64. |
| — | low | Expired codes and abandoned dynamic clients piled up. | Pruned on registration. |

### Found later by fuzzing

The fuzz package (`host-src/packages/fuzz`, `just fuzz`) added a seeded mutation fuzzer for
every parser and a property test of the SQL authorizer. Every parser ran clean, and so did the
whole suite under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer. The property test found two
real holes:

| # | severity | finding | fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | high | `INSERT INTO open SELECT * FROM secret` copied a table the strategy had no grant on. SQLite's transfer optimization copies raw rows without asking the authorizer about the columns it reads. | Before a box's statement runs, the host walks its compiled program: every table or index it opens must belong to a table the strategy grants something on, and a program that copies raw rows (RowData) may only read tables the strategy reads in full. This check doesn't depend on the authorizer. |
| F2 | medium | A write SQLite interrupted (a runaway recursive DELETE past the step budget), or `INSERT OR ROLLBACK`, rolled back the request's whole transaction. Later statements then committed one at a time, so a request that answered 5xx could still leave writes behind. | `OR ROLLBACK` is refused like REPLACE. A session that sees SQLite leave its transaction refuses every later statement and fails its commit, so the request fails and nothing of it lands. |

### Second review, 2026-10-27

A correctness review of the newest code (network, outbox, scheduler, keys, mail, shutdown) and a
product-readiness review found these; each is fixed, with a test where one could be written.

| # | severity | finding | fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | high | A path value of `..` in an external call made curl walk out of the declared method path, with the service's credential attached. | Path values `.`, `..`, and empty are refused, and curl runs with `--path-as-is`. |
| R2 | medium-high | The dispatcher read the 50 oldest pending elements; 50 stuck elements of one service starved every other service in that outbox. | Delivery goes service by service, each from its own oldest element. |
| R3 | medium | A sent element whose outcome couldn't be written (a busy database) stayed pending and was sent again, and one failing location stopped the tick for every later one. | The outcome write is retried; each location is delivered on its own, inside its own try. |
| R4 | medium | External calls ignored the request's deadline (a fixed 10 s, wasm deadlines can't interrupt a host call), so slow services could pin every box thread. | Calls get what's left of the request's time, at most 10 s. |
| R5 | medium | An internal call into a box sharing the caller's database waited on the caller's own write lock, then failed. | Refused at upload: a box shares a database or calls the box, not both. |
| R6 | medium-low | A timer fire that threw stayed "running" forever. | Released however the fire ends. |
| R7 | medium-low | Queued jobs ran during destruction against freed members. | Shutdown drops jobs that never started and joins the pool before tearing anything down. |
| R8 | medium-low | An exception inside `goober_sql` or `goober_call` would unwind through wasmtime's frames. | Both catch everything and hand the box an error. |
| R9 | low | The SMTP thread could die on a filesystem error; a failed write left the password file. | Caught, and the file is always removed. |
| R10 | low | Outbox elements of a removed box were never delivered or listed. | Storage is scanned every minute for outboxes. |
| R11 | low | A failed cron fire counted as done, so it was never caught up. | Only completed fires are recorded. |
| R12 | low | Two `key_set`s of one key shared a temporary file. | Each write has its own. |
| P1 | blocker | Caddy's health check names the upstream address, which no domain resolves, so the site went 503. | `/goober/health` answers whatever the Host. |
| P2 | high | Big pushes from the operator hit the per-address limit and stopped halfway. | Operators are limited only when their token fails. |
| P3 | medium | Public POST routes had only the global budget, so a form that mails could be used to flood. | Anonymous writes have their own budget (20 a minute per address). |

## Not fixed, and why

| # | finding | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I1 (rest) | The identity and consent screens share an origin with box pages and documents, which 013 says they must not. | **Q24.** Moving them to a host-owned origin is a trust-boundary decision. The Sec-Fetch requirement closes the silent-consent path in current browsers; a script can still lure a click. |
| C5 | Anonymous requests walk the tenant's tree on disk (`_data/`, 404s, listings) with no cache. | A performance issue more than a hole; caching per WAL commit is PROGRESS.md work. |
| C9 | Public attachments are enumerable by id, including images used only on private pages. | By design for now (Q20): `public` means anyone can fetch it. Restricted pages should use `members` attachments. |
| B14 | Boxes may still set `cache-control`, `location`, `access-control-*`, and similar headers. | Waits for host-owned CORS and the status vocabulary (037, PROGRESS.md Phase 5). |
| B8 (rest) | No per-IP connection cap or per-tenant concurrency cap. | Behind Caddy every connection is one address; per-tenant caps belong with quotas (390). |
| — | Raw `text/html` from a box runs with no CSP. | Products need their own script; the separate origin of Q24 is the real boundary. |
| — | Cookie tossing when `cookie-secure` is false. | Only local mode turns it off. |

## Checked and found sound

The reviewers traced these and found nothing:

- **Box boundary.** Pointer checks, imports, header rules, and hiding credentials from boxes.
- **SQL authorizer.** It denies PRAGMA (including the `pragma_*` functions), ATTACH, DDL, transactions, and the host's and SQLite's tables. It doesn't trust CTE names, it authorizes RETURNING reads, and it allows only pure functions.
- **Tenant databases.** No cross-tenant paths.
- **WAL and stores.** Path checks everywhere.
- **Operator tokens.** Random, hashed, compared in constant time, and kept out of logs.
- **The validator.** No parser differential.
- **The Beast parser.** Header limits and read deadlines.
- **Agents.** Agent tokens are refused where they should be.
- **Identity.** Host and tenant identities stay apart; no cross-realm tokens; PKCE, codes, and refresh rotation behave; no races under the realm locks.
- **Screens and sessions.** Redirect URI matching; escaping and framing on the screens; no session fixation; cookie attributes.
- **Logs.** No token reaches a log.
- **Markdown and Mustache.** The renderer escapes raw HTML and filters URL schemes; Mustache escapes everything but the host's `body`.
- **Content and attachments.** Path normalization and private segments, and draft and requirement checks before every other way of reaching a page. Attachments are sniffed, `nosniff`, and SVG and PDF are downloads under a sandbox CSP. The multipart parser runs in linear time.
