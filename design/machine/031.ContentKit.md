# The content kit: posts, sitemaps, and deploying with checks

A design, built the same day. On 2026-10-29 the engineers of a site the host serves sent two lists. The first
was eight requests ranked by tokens saved. The second asked for markdown blog posts, which
took them 14 minutes and three hand-edited files for one post. This is what goober does about
both.

## 1. sitemap.xml and llms.txt

**When the host serves them.** Unless a file of the tenant's or a box route answers the path,
the host serves `/sitemap.xml` and `/llms.txt`, built from the pages' front matter.

**Which pages are listed.** Pages that are:
- published, not drafts;
- public, with no `requirements`;
- not redirects;
- not `noindex: true`;
- not `sitemap: false`.

**What each entry holds:**
- `loc` is the page's `canonical` field when it is absolute, else the origin and the URL;
- `lastmod` is `updated`, else `date`.

**llms.txt** follows llmstxt.org:
- the title (`site.title`, else the domain);
- the description (`site.description`), as a quote;
- a `## Pages` list of links with each page's `description` or `summary`.

**A box's own sitemap** (a box's own places pages, say) joins by name: `"sitemaps":
["/places/sitemap.xml"]` in `_data/site.json`. `/sitemap.xml` then becomes a sitemap index of
those sitemaps and `/sitemap-pages.xml`, which lists the pages. That box's sitemap stops
changing with every new page.

## 2. Posts

**The model.** A post is a markdown page with `template: "post"`. Its index is a listing.
1. **Once:** `_data/site.json` with `title`, `description`, and `author`.
2. **Once:** the index, `page_put /updates` with `{list: "updates", "list-template": "post",
   "per-page": 10}`.
3. **Each post:** `page_put /updates/<slug>` with `format: "markdown"`, `template: "post"`,
   and `{date, summary, author?, tags, image?, draft?}`.

**What was added:**
- **A built-in `post` template:** the title, then a byline (`author`, else `site.author`), the
  date, the image, the body, and the tags.
- **A BlogPosting.** Unless a post has its own `json_ld`, the host builds one from its front
  matter:
  - headline, datePublished, dateModified, description;
  - author, as a Person;
  - image, as an absolute URL;
  - keywords, mainEntityOfPage, and publisher.
- **The built-in `base` template** now emits:
  - a canonical link;
  - `rel=prev` and `rel=next` on a listing's pages;
  - Open Graph and Twitter tags (`og:type` is `article` for a post);
  - `meta name="author"`;
  - `{{{json_ld}}}`. Before, a page's `json_ld` was dropped unless the tenant's own template
    printed it.
- **Canonical URLs.** A listing's later pages are canonical with their `?page=`.
- **`list-template`** on a listing keeps only the pages written with that template.
- **Each feed entry** carries its author.
- **Every listing has feeds**, unless its front matter says `feed: false` (before, only
  `feed: true` had one). The human asked on 2026-10-29 that each indexable list have a feed.
  Both formats are served from the same entries:
  - Atom at `feed.xml`, as before;
  - RSS 2.0 at `rss.xml`, with `pubDate` in RFC 822, `dc:creator`, and `category`. RSS is
    still the name RSS-to-email and automation tools ask for; every reader takes both.

  The base template advertises both with `rel=alternate`.

**The engineers' gaps, one by one:**
- **`page_list` ignored `path`.** Its parameter is `dir`. `path` and `prefix` are now aliases
  (§ 6).
- **`page_get` has an etag**, and `page_put` takes `if_etag`, where `"none"` means only a new
  page.
- **Two etags, labelled (2026-10-30).** A page has two: its stored file's, which `if_etag`
  compares, and its served body's, after templates and partials, which HTTP clients see. Every
  tool now names which it gives: `file_etag` (`page_put`, `page_get`, `list_etags`, the partial
  and template tools, `content_deploy`'s items) and `body_etag` (`page_render`, `url_check`,
  `content_deploy`'s items). The old `etag` fields stay as they were: the file's everywhere but
  `page_render`, where it is the body's.
- **Templates take `if_etag` (2026-10-30).** `template_get` gives this tenant's own stored
  template's `file_etag` (`"none"` when the text comes from `_host` or the built-ins),
  `template_list` gives `file_etags` by name, and `template_put` and `template_delete` take
  `if_etag` as `page_put` does. `list_etags` takes `_templates/NAME.html` and
  `_partials/NAME.html`.
- **Writes name their changes (2026-10-30).** A tool call that writes answers `change_ids` (and
  `change_id` when there is one): the change log's rows (028 § 5) made by that call. A source
  commit isn't in the change log; its revision number is its pin.
- **The tenant** may be left out wherever it's required and only one is possible (§ 6).
- **Drafts** preview with `page_render {drafts: true}`, as before.
- **Moving hand-written documents** is in `site_guide`: put each one again as a post. That
  replaces the document at its URL. Then delete the hand-kept `updates/feed.xml`.

**Engineer B's second list** (the same day):
- **A head slot.** A template wrapped in a layout puts tags between `<!--head-->` and
  `<!--/head-->`, and they go to the layout's `{{{head}}}`; the built-in base prints it in its
  `<head>`.
- **JSON in scripts.** `{{{json.title}}}` (any plain value) is the value as JSON, with `<`, `>`,
  `&`, U+2028, and U+2029 escaped, so it's safe inside `<script type="application/ld+json">`.
- **Host-only slots.** `head` and `json` are host-only: a page's front matter or a box's data
  can't set them.
- **Feed images.** Each feed entry carries its image: an Atom `rel=enclosure` link, and RSS
  `media:content`.
- **`template_delete`**, and `template_put`'s description now says how to stage a template:
  preview with `page_render`'s unsaved `templates`, or put it under a new name on a draft page.

## 3. Compact output

**What's compact now:**
- **`page_render`** answers status, kind, file, bytes, sha256, the etag, and the checks of § 5.
  The HTML (and a feed's XML) comes only with `verbose: true`.
- **`source_build`, `source_deploy`, and `source_job`** answer a job without its log: its size
  and last three lines. The whole log comes when the job failed, or with `verbose`.
- **`source_diff`** answers each file with lines added and removed. The diff comes with
  `verbose`.

**`page_get` keeps its body**, since it's how a page is read before an edit. It now carries
the etag.

## 4. content_deploy and list_etags

`content_deploy {pages, files, verify?, strict?}`:
- **The items.** Pages are `page_put`'s arguments. Files are `{path, text | base64}`. Either
  may add `sha256` (of what was sent) and `if_etag`.
- **Before.** Every item is checked: its body or bytes against its `sha256`, its file against
  its `if_etag`, and a page's arguments as `page_put` checks them. If any item fails, nothing is
  written.
- **Then** every item is written.
- **After.** Unless `verify` is false, each URL is fetched through the host's own listener,
  signed out, as a visitor gets it.
- **The answer** is one line per item, plus `ok`: status, content type, `file_etag` and
  `body_etag`, the ld+json count, and any problem (§ 5), and `items`, the same per URL as
  fields. A file whose served bytes differ from what was written fails.

`list_etags {paths}` gives each URL's or file's answering file and `file_etag`.

## 5. Checks

**What a served HTML page is checked for:**
- an unrendered `{{`;
- more than one ld+json, or one that doesn't parse;
- an empty `<p>`;
- an `http:` link (except to this machine in local mode);
- a raw markdown link (`](`).

**`strict` adds:**
- exactly one ld+json;
- a title of 50 to 60 characters;
- a description of 120 to 160 characters.

**The tools that run these checks:**
- **`url_check {urls}`** works on any URL of this host's sites. A focused operator (030) can
  check only their own tenants. It's for post-deploy checks, in place of the curl scripts.
- **`page_lint {paths?, strict?}`** runs over every listed page (the sitemap's set) or the
  paths given.

**Builds already validate.** `source_build` has run every box through upload's own check
since 020, so a green build deploys. The new piece is the page lint.

## 6. Stable tool schemas

**Aliases.** A tool may declare old argument names (`page_list` takes `path` and `prefix` for
`dir`). They're renamed before the call.

**Unknown arguments** are dropped, and an object answer names them in `ignored_arguments`. A
filter that didn't apply is no longer silent.

**Schema hashes.** `tools/list` gives each tool `_meta["goober/schema"]`, the first 12 hex
digits of its input schema's SHA-256. `tool_schemas` lists every hash and alias. A changed hash
means re-reading `tools/list`.

**The default tenant.** Where a tool requires `tenant` and only one is possible, it's filled
in. Only one is possible when the host serves one tenant, or the operator is focused on one.
A tenant that is optional means something by its absence (every tenant, the system's keys, a
box project), so it's never filled in.

## 7. For the human

**6. Versioned assets pinned by hash.** Partials (027) are already tenant-level and keep ten
versions. Pinning means an include names a version, like `<!--#include card@3f2a…-->`, and
the page keeps rendering that version until it's moved. Components with parameters (a card
grid) need partials that take arguments. Both change the include syntax 027 settled.
Recommended: pins by version hash, and parameters as `key="value"` pairs on the marker.

**7. An approvals ledger.** The request is an append-only record of the operator's verbatim
approval, its time, and its scope (a commit, an etag, a standing rule), with
`verify_approval(scope)`. As asked, an agent writes the ledger, so it records what the agent
says the human said. 305 and 410 make approval a human act in the UI. Recommended: approvals
are recorded in the panel by a person, or by a confirm link mailed to the operator, and agents
only read them with `verify_approval`. This is a trust boundary, so it waits for the human.

## 8. Tests

- **The blog** (`content_test`):
  - one `page_put` per post gives the byline, the meta description, OG tags, a canonical link,
    and exactly one BlogPosting that parses;
  - the listing is newest first, posts only, drafts hidden, two a page, with rel prev/next and
    per-page canonicals;
  - the feed has the author;
  - the sitemap has lastmod, and llms.txt the descriptions;
  - the sitemap index names a box's sitemap;
  - a tenant's own `sitemap.xml` wins.
- **Etags:** stale ones refused and fresh ones written; `none`; the `page_list` alias;
  `ignored_arguments`; `list_etags`; `tool_schemas`.
- **content_deploy:** a bad sha writes nothing; a page and a CSV deploy and verify; problems
  are flagged. `url_check` refuses other hosts. `page_lint`'s strict lengths.
- **Scope:** a required tenant is filled in when focused on one; an optional one never is.
