# Invites, consent, and mail preferences

A design, built the same day. On 2026-10-29 the human asked that invites be a host feature,
part of identity:
- The host owns the template and keeps a record of every invite.
- An explicit decline is recorded, and from then on only a human can re-enable that address:
  the operator, or the person with a statement that they want mail again.
- An invite carries what accepting grants: roles, and acceptance of the site's terms
  ("by continuing, you accept the terms").
- The host keeps a table of mail preferences.
- The design leans toward the strictest reading of the mail laws, because mail matters.

Putting the host in charge of invites limits what a misbehaving box can do: it can't mail an
address that declined, grant a role it wasn't given, or hide how to say no.

The human chose:
- **A decline blocks one tenant.** It stops that tenant's invites and box mail. Other tenants
  on the host are untouched.
- **Boxes may invite, with declared roles.** A box asks through an `invites` service. The
  invite carries only roles the box's manifest declares and the host config allows for that
  tenant.
- **Consent by category.** Every box mail has a category with its own rule. Lists need a
  recorded opt-in. Cold outreach stays possible, but one-to-one, capped, and ended for good by
  one opt-out.
- **One reminder.** An unanswered invite gets one reminder after 7 days and lapses at 30.
  The address can't be invited again for 90 days after that.

This replaces the invite of 021 § 1 (an unproved account made by a grant) for anything that
mails. `role_grant` to an address without an account still makes the waiting account and
mails nothing (§ 9).

## 1. The words

- **An invite** asks someone to join a site. It's a host record, mailed by the host. It's not
  a calendar invite (`invite` on mail `send`, 783 § 3), which stays as it is.
- **A block** marks an address the tenant may not mail: it declined an invite, unsubscribed
  from everything, or was blocked by an operator. Only a human lifts it (§ 6).
- **A list** is mail someone subscribes to, like a daily digest. A box's mail service declares
  its lists, and each list needs an opt-in (§ 4).
- **Consent** is the record of someone saying yes (or no) to a list: when, how, and what they
  were shown.
- **The postal address** is the sender's physical address. CAN-SPAM requires it in commercial
  mail, and CASL requires contact information. The host config carries it per tenant (§ 8).

## 2. Invites

### 2.1 Making one

An operator calls `invite_send`, or a box calls its `invites` service's `send`:

| field | |
| --- | --- |
| `email` | the one address invited |
| `roles` | roles granted on acceptance: tenant roles (`member`, `editor/blog`). A box may name only its declared roles (§ 2.6) |
| `lists` | lists joined on acceptance, by name. The invite page shows each one |
| `note` | optional, up to 500 characters of plain text, shown as a quote from the inviter |
| `inviter_name` | optional display name, up to 64 characters. The page also shows who really sent it (§ 2.2) |
| `return` | optional path on the site to land on after accepting, like `/welcome` |

The host refuses an invite, and records nothing, when:
- the address is blocked for the tenant (`why: "blocked"`);
- the address already has a proved account in the realm (`"member"`). Use `role_grant`;
- an invite to it is open (`"open"`, with its id);
- one lapsed or was revoked less than 90 days ago (`"cooling"`, with the day it ends);
- the tenant has no postal address in the host config (`"no_postal_address"`);
- the tenant sent `invites.daily-max` invites in the last day (default 50), or this inviter
  sent `invites.per-inviter-daily` (default 10);
- a role or list isn't allowed (§ 2.6).

Otherwise the host stores the invite and queues its mail. A box's invite is an outbox element
(verb `INVITE`) in its request's transaction: the host stores and mails it only after the
request commits, so a failed request leaves no invite. The call checks what it can at once
(blocked, member, open, the postal address); a cooling address or a daily limit reached
meanwhile makes the element a dead letter.

### 2.2 Who invited

The invite names who asked, and a box can't put words in anyone's mouth:
- **An operator:** "the team at {site}", or `inviter_name`.
- **A box, in a request from a signed-in person:** that person. The page shows their
  `inviter_name` if given, and always their address: "Ann (ann@example.com) invited you".
- **A box, with nobody signed in** (a timer, a mail route): the site itself, "{site} invited
  you". `inviter_name` is refused, so a timer can't sign an invite with a member's name.

CASL exempts a referral only when it names the person who referred, so naming the real
inviter keeps an invite on the right side of it.

### 2.3 The mail

The host sends the `invite` template (022), overridable per tenant like every host mail. Its
values: `site`, `email`, `inviter`, `inviter_email`, `note`, `roles`, `lists` (title and
description each), `terms_url`, `privacy_url`, `accept_url`, `decline_url`,
`postal_address`, `expires` (a date), and `reminder` (true for the reminder).

Whatever a tenant's template says, the host checks the rendered mail before it goes:
- **The text and HTML parts each contain `accept_url` and `decline_url`.** Otherwise the
  default template is used instead, and the outbox element notes why.
- **The footer is the host's:** the postal address, why the mail came ("{inviter} invited you
  to {site}. You'll get one reminder, then nothing."), and the decline link. It's added after
  the template, so no template can drop it.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`** (RFC 8058) point at the decline link.
  Gmail's and Apple's unsubscribe buttons then decline the invite.

Invite mail is counted against the tenant's `mail.daily-max`, in the same ledger as box mail.

### 2.4 The links

`accept_url` is `https://{site}/identity/invite/{id}/{mac}`, and `decline_url` adds
`/decline`. The MAC is an HMAC under the host's reply key over the tenant, the invite id, and
the address, so the link can't be guessed or moved to another invite.

- **A GET never changes anything.** Mail scanners open every link.
- **The accept page** (GET) shows:
  - who invited, the note, the roles in plain words, and each list;
  - "By continuing, you accept {site}'s [terms] and [privacy policy]", when the tenant has
    them (§ 8);
  - a **Continue** button and a **No thanks** link.

  **Continue** (POST, same origin) does one of three things:
  - signed in as the invited address: it accepts at once (§ 2.5);
  - signed in as someone else: it says so, and offers sign-out;
  - otherwise: it mails a sign-in code to the invited address and sends the browser to
    `/identity/sign-in/code`, whose form carries the invite's id and MAC and says the same "by
    continuing" line. A good code signs in and accepts.

  The code is what proves the address. The link alone never signs anyone in, so a forwarded
  invite can't be accepted by someone else.
- **The decline page** (GET) asks: "Don't invite me to {site} again?" **Yes, stop** (POST)
  declines (§ 2.5). A one-click POST from a mail client (`List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) declines
  the same way.
- **A lapsed, revoked, or answered invite's links** show what happened ("You declined this on
  …") and offer nothing else. The decline link of a lapsed invite still blocks: saying no is
  always possible.

Signing in to the site any other way, with an invite open, sends the person to the accept page
before their `return`. Nothing is accepted without that page.

### 2.5 Answers

| answer | the host records | and |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **accepted** | `answered_at`, the session's request id | The account is proved (021). The roles are granted, checked again against § 2.6. The terms are accepted (§ 8). The lists are opted in, with the invite as the evidence (§ 4). The `invite-accepted` identity event fires. |
| **declined** | `answered_at`, how (page, or one-click) | A block for the tenant, `how: "invite_declined"` (§ 6). The `invite-declined` event fires. Nothing is mailed. |
| **lapsed** | 30 days after it was made, unanswered | Nothing is mailed. The address cools for 90 days. |
| **revoked** | an operator, or the box that sent it | Nothing is mailed. Its links say it was withdrawn. The address cools for 90 days. |

**The reminder.** 7 days after the invite, if it's unanswered and the address isn't blocked,
the host sends the `invite` template once more, with `reminder: true`. It never sends a
third.

**Identity events (021 § 3).** `invite-accepted` and `invite-declined` join `first-verified`
and `signed-in`. A box hears them through `routes.identity`. The body adds `invite_id` and the
`box` that sent the invite, or `null` for an operator. `invite-declined` is called as nobody,
since the person never signed in.

### 2.6 What a box may grant

A box declares the service:

```json
"external": {"club": {"invites": {"roles": ["member"], "lists": ["digest"]}}}
```

- **`roles`** are tenant roles. At upload, and at each call, every role must also be in the
  host config's `tenants.$tenant.invites.roles` for each tenant the box serves. A host with no
  such entry lets boxes grant no roles.
- **`lists`** must be lists the same box's `mail` service declares (§ 4).
- **Methods:**
  - `send`, as in § 2.1;
  - `revoke {invite_id}`, for invites this box sent;
  - `status {email}`, which answers `{state, invite_id?, can_invite, why?}` without the note
    or who invited.
- **No `role_grant`, no unblock, and no list of who declined.** A box sees only its own
  invites' outcomes.

An operator's `invite_send` may name any tenant role. It still can't invite a blocked
address.

### 2.7 The record

Each realm's database gains `invites`:

| column | |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | never reused |
| `email` | the address, as typed, normalized |
| `state` | `open`, `accepted`, `declined`, `lapsed`, `revoked` |
| `roles`, `lists` | JSON arrays |
| `note`, `inviter_name`, `inviter_email`, `inviter_kind` | `operator`, `person`, or `site` |
| `box` | the box that sent it, or NULL |
| `return_path` | |
| `created_at`, `reminded_at`, `answered_at`, `expires_at` | |
| `answer_how` | `page`, `one_click`, `code`, `session` |
| `terms_sha256` | the terms page's hash when it was sent |

The operator reads them with `invite_list` (by state, address, or box) and `invite_get`.
Invites are kept as long as the realm. They are the record the human asked for.

## 3. Categories of mail

Every mail a box sends has a `category` on `send`. The host checks the category's rule when
the box calls `send`, and again when the outbox delivers, since consent can change between
the two.

| category | for | who may get it | limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `reply` | answering mail the person sent | the address that sent the message named by `in_reply_to`, in the last 30 days (018 § 3) | `mail.reply-per-address-daily` (20) |
| `transactional` | receipts, notices about the person's own account or request | a proved account of the realm, or an address that mailed the tenant in the last 90 days | `mail.per-address-daily` (3) |
| `confirm` | a confirmation link for an address someone just typed (a lead form, a booking) | any unblocked address, only from an `http` route (a person is there) | 1 per address a day, 3 in 30 days |
| `outreach` | a cold, one-to-one message | any unblocked address that hasn't opted out of outreach | 1 per address in 30 days, unless they replied (then it's `reply`); `mail.outreach-daily-max` per tenant (default 50) |
| `list` (with `list`) | a list's mail: digests, newsletters | addresses opted in to that list (§ 4) | `mail.per-address-daily` (3) |

- **A block stops every category.** Sign-in codes are the only mail a blocked address gets,
  and only when that person asks for one.
- **A missing `category`** is inferred, strictly: `reply` if the threading checks out, else
  `transactional` if the address is an account or wrote in, else `outreach`. No inference
  gives `list` or `confirm`. Existing boxes keep working, held to the stricter rule.
- **The order of refusals.** A mail's own faults (no subject, a bad invite, a stranger's
  attachment) are reported before a consent refusal, so a box fixes its mail first.
- **"Wrote in"** is the received ledger of mail in (024), now kept 90 days instead of 30. A
  reply's thread still has to be from the last 30.
- **The postal address is required** for `outreach` and `list` mail, and for invites. Without
  `tenants.$tenant.postal-address`, those sends are refused in the call, with the config key
  named.
- **The footer is the host's** for every category: who sent it, why ("you're getting this
  because you subscribed to {list}", "because you wrote to us", "because you have an account
  on {site}", "we wrote to you once; we won't again unless you reply"), the postal address
  where required, and the unsubscribe or preferences link. It's appended after the tenant's
  layout, as in § 2.3.
- **Headers.** Every box mail keeps `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`. List mail
  adds `List-Id: <{list}.{tenant}.{site}>` (RFC 2919).

Why these rules:
- **CAN-SPAM:** an opt-out in every commercial mail, honored at once, where the law allows
  10 business days. A postal address, and From addresses on the tenant's own domains.
- **CASL:** express consent for lists, with a record of it. Implied consent only from a real
  relationship: an account, or the person writing in. The sender is identified and reachable.
- **GDPR and ePrivacy:** consent that is specific (per list), informed (the page shows what
  it means), and recorded, and that is as easy to withdraw as to give.
- **Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender rules:** one-click unsubscribe, honored well within 2 days.

Cold outreach is the one category the strict laws don't bless. It stays because the human's
experiments include agent outreach to businesses (READINESS). It's held to one message per
address per month, the opt-out ends it for good, and the footer says it's a one-off.

## 4. Lists and consent

A box's `mail` service declares its lists:

```json
"external": {"mail": {"mail": {"from-name": "Beef Share",
  "lists": {"digest": {"title": "The daily digest", "about": "One email a day at 7am about what the group said."}}}}}
```

- A list name is `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`. `title` is up to 80 characters, `about` up to 300.
- The tenant's lists are the union over the boxes serving it. Two boxes declaring the same
  name must agree on its title, or the second upload is refused.

**Ways to opt in.** Each records who, what, when, and how:

| how | evidence |
| --- | --- |
| `invite` | an accepted invite listing the list; its id |
| `account` | a signed-in person turned the list on, on their account page |
| `confirmed` | the person clicked the confirm link of a `subscribe` mail and pressed **Yes** |
| `operator` | an operator recorded consent given elsewhere (paper, an event), with the statement in their words. Shown as such |

A box can't record consent itself. It asks with the mail service's `subscribe {email, list}`.
The host then sends the `list-confirm` template, with a link
(`/goober/list/{tenant}/{hash}/{list}/{mac}`) to a page showing the list's title and about and
a **Yes, subscribe me** button. That's double opt-in. It's limited to one `subscribe` mail per
address and list in 30 days, counted when asked, and never to a blocked address. A tenant's
`list-confirm` template that drops the link is replaced by the default.

**Ways to opt out:**
- the unsubscribe link of any list mail, which drops that list. Its page also offers "stop all
  mail from {site}", which is a block;
- the person's account page;
- the preferences page (§ 5);
- an operator.

Opting out of a list is final for that list until the person opts in again, by any way in the
table except `operator`. An operator may record consent, but never re-subscribe someone who
opted out.

**Reading consent.** The mail service's `preferences {email}` answers:
- `blocked`;
- each list: `in`, `out`, or `none`;
- `relationship`: `account`, `wrote_in`, or `none`;
- `outreach`: `allowed`, `used` (with the date), or `opted_out`.

A box can ask before it writes. Nothing in the answer says why someone opted out.

**The record**, in `storage/mail/consent.db`, keyed by tenant and a hash of the address (as
`unsubscribed.db` was):
- `consent`: the current state per address and list: `in` or `out`, `at`, `how`, and
  `evidence` (JSON);
- `consent_log`: every change, append-only, for proof. Kept 5 years, CASL's practical horizon;
- `blocks`: § 6;
- `outreach`: the first and latest outreach per address, for the 30-day rule.

The address itself isn't stored. To show an operator "who opted in", the host matches the
hashes against the tenant's accounts and open invites. A list of who opted out can't be
produced, by design.

## 5. The preferences page

`/goober/mail/{tenant}/{hash}/{mac}` on the tenant's site, or `…/list.{name}` for a list's
mail. It's what every footer and `List-Unsubscribe` header links to. The old
`/goober/unsubscribe/…` links still work and show the same page.
- **GET** shows the lists this address is in, each with **Leave**; **Stop all mail from
  {site}** (a block); and, when signed in as that address, a link to the account page.
- **POST** with `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, to the link a list mail carried, leaves that
  list. On a non-list mail it blocks.
- **The link only ever reduces mail.** Joining a list or lifting a block takes a signed-in
  session (the account page) or a confirm mail, because a link can be forwarded.

The account page (`/identity/account`) gains a **Mail** section: every list of the tenant with
a toggle (opting in here records `how: "account"`), and the block, if any (§ 6).

## 6. Blocks

A block is one row per tenant and address hash:
- `at`, `how` (`invite_declined`, `unsubscribed`, `complaint`, `operator`), and `evidence`;
- `lifted_at`, `lifted_by`, `statement`, once lifted.

`unsubscribed.db`'s rows become blocks with `how: "unsubscribed"`, once, at startup.

**What a block stops:**
- every box mail, in the call and again at delivery;
- every invite;
- list confirmations.

Sign-in codes the person asks for still go, and they can still sign in.

**Lifting a block takes a human. Either:**
- **The person.** Signed in (so the address is proved by a code), on the account page:
  "You asked {site} not to email you on {date}." Lifting it needs a checked box, "I'm asking
  {site} to email me again", and **Allow mail again**. The statement is recorded verbatim with
  the session's request id. Lifting doesn't join any list.
- **An operator, in the panel** (028), with a statement of why (at least 20 characters, like
  "Asked by phone on 11/2 to rejoin the digest"): the Operators view's **Lift a mail block**,
  which posts `/api/write/unblock` with `confirm`. `host:owner` and `host:operator` may, within
  their scope (030); an observer can't. There's no MCP tool for this. Grok, or any agent with an
  operator token, can't lift a block. The change log records it as `mail_block_lift`.

Lifting leaves the history: the block row keeps its dates, and `consent_log` has both
changes.

**Complaints and bounces** (026 M2) become blocks with `how: "complaint"` once SES's
notifications reach the host. That isn't built yet (§ 11).

## 7. Operator tools

| tool | does |
| --- | --- |
| `invite_send` | § 2.1, as the operator |
| `invite_list`, `invite_get` | the record, with each invite's state and dates |
| `invite_revoke` | withdraws an open invite |
| `mail_preferences` | one address's state for a tenant: block, lists, relationship, outreach |
| `mail_consent_record` | records consent given elsewhere (`how: "operator"`), with a statement. It can't override an `out` |
| `mail_lists` | the tenant's lists, which box declares each, and how many are in |

`mail_unsubscribed` stays as a shim: checking still works, and adding makes a block. Removing
answers "lift blocks in the panel".

## 8. The host config, per tenant

A new top-level section:

```json
"tenants": {
  "shop": {
    "postal-address": "Beef Share, 1234 Main St, Kansas City, MO 64105",
    "terms": "/terms", "privacy": "/privacy",
    "invites": {"roles": ["member"], "daily-max": 50, "per-inviter-daily": 10}
  }
}
```

- **`postal-address`** is up to 200 characters on one line. It's required for outreach, lists,
  and invites.
- **`terms` and `privacy`** are paths of pages on the tenant's site.
- **Terms acceptance.** When `terms` is set:
  - the sign-in code page says "By continuing, you accept {site}'s terms and privacy policy",
    with links;
  - a good code records, in the realm's `terms_accepted` (user, path, the SHA-256 of the page's
    file at that moment, at, how), that this person accepted that version;
  - a page whose hash has changed since the person's last acceptance is accepted again at
    their next sign-in, under the same line (the line doesn't say "updated": the page can't
    know who is signing in before the code);
  - an invite's acceptance records it too, with `how: "invite"`.
- **`invites.roles`** is what boxes may grant (§ 2.6).
- **`mail.outreach-daily-max`** joins the host-wide mail limits.

## 9. What changes for what exists

- **`role_grant` to a new address** still makes an unproved account with the role waiting,
  and mails nothing. To mail someone, use `invite_send`.
- **`send` without a category** is inferred (§ 3). Outreach now means once a month per
  address, not 3 a day, and needs the postal address. A tenant that sends outreach needs
  `tenants.$tenant.postal-address` in the host config before its next send.
- **`mail_suppressed`** now asks the block table. The unsubscribe link still works, and leads
  to the preferences page.
- **The default layout** loses its own unsubscribe line. The host's footer replaces it, so
  it's never printed twice.
- **One-click on a mail that isn't a list's** blocks the address, as unsubscribing always did.
  On a list's mail it leaves that list only.

## 10. Tests

- **identity:**
  - an invite is made, refused for each `why`, accepted by code, and accepted by an existing
    session;
  - roles are granted on acceptance and refused when the config dropped them;
  - terms acceptance is recorded with the page's hash, and again when the hash changes;
  - lapse and cooling, the reminder sent exactly once, and revocation.
- **consent:**
  - each category's rule, and the inference;
  - opting in each way, with its evidence;
  - opting out per list, and a block stopping everything;
  - lifting needs a statement, and an operator can't override an `out`;
  - `unsubscribed.db` migrated;
  - the 5-year log.
- **The host:**
  - a box's invite carries only declared and configured roles;
  - an invite's mail has the links and the host footer even when a tenant's template drops
    them;
  - one-click decline from `List-Unsubscribe-Post`;
  - a GET never changes state;
  - a forwarded link can't sign anyone in;
  - delivery re-checks consent;
  - a lapsed invite's decline link still blocks;
  - lifting a block: from the account page with the statement, and from the panel only.
    There's no MCP tool.

## 11. Left out

- **SES bounces and complaints as blocks (026 M2).** The table and its `how` are ready. The
  SNS endpoint, with signature checks, isn't built.
- **Invites to many people in one call.** A box loops, within its limits.
- **A host-wide block** ("never from any site on this host"). The human chose per tenant.
- **Data subject requests** (export or erase one person). Today an operator deletes the
  account. The hashes in `consent.db` stay, so an opt-out is never forgotten.
- **CASL's one-message referral rule.** CASL's referral exemption covers one message, and the
  reminder is a second. A host config `invites.reminder: false` would turn reminders off for a
  tenant with Canadian members. It isn't built; flagged for the human.
